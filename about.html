About.

I photograph the shows, games and places I end up in, and keep a journal of them here. It’s documentation of my own life, taken about as seriously as I’d take anyone else’s.

Franklin, TN → Chicago · hello@heartslibrary.com

What I shoot

Small rooms with loud bands, mostly. Ballparks. Gardens and zoos and whatever is out the window of wherever I’ve just moved to.

Most of it comes off a Sony RX100 VII, because it fits in a jacket pocket. Some off a G Master lens. A lot off a phone, at the wrong hour. A frame either held something or it didn’t, and the gear has never once been the reason.

How I got here

I drew sports stars as a kid and studied illustration at California College of the Arts. Then years of labeling data sets at Postmates and Twitter, and designing internal tools for healthcare. All of it was practice at attention. The camera is where that finally went — it asks less of me than drawing did, and it goes where I go.

Why this site

Working in technology means wondering whether the systems we build have empathy. Mostly they don’t, and that matters more now that AI is being deployed where substantial civilian death is expected. You can write a constitution for a model. You cannot write empathy into one — empathy takes time, and time is the thing being optimized away.

I’m not claiming a photograph fixes that. But it takes time to look at something properly. So this site keeps track: a page per outing, the writing sitting next to the frames. It’s what gets me to carry the camera.

— Cody Heart
