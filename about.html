About.

I photograph the shows, games and places I end up in, and keep a journal of them here. It’s documentation of my own life, taken about as seriously as I’d take anyone else’s.

Franklin, TN → Chicago · hello@heartslibrary.com

How I got here

I drew sports stars as a kid, studied illustration at California College of the Arts, then drifted out of making images into operations at Postmates and Twitter, labeling the data sets that trained early machine learning models. Taught myself what was then called UX. Have spent my working life since designing internal tools for healthcare.

The camera is how I got back to making images. It asks less of me than drawing did and it goes where I go — a show, a ballgame, a garden in Normandy. What ends up here is whatever was worth keeping afterwards.

Some of it came off a G Master lens. More of it came off a phone, in a pocket, at the wrong hour. I stopped sorting those into different piles a while ago — a frame either held something or it didn’t, and the gear has never once been the reason. What matters more is that something gets written down next to it.

This site is the reason any of that happens. Nobody commissioned it and nobody is waiting on it, so building it properly — a real page per outing, the writing sitting next to the frames — is the only thing standing between me and a camera roll I never look at again. Setting a high bar for it is what gets me to carry the camera, and shooting more is the only way I have ever got any better.

Why this site

Working in technology means spending a lot of time wondering whether the systems we build have empathy. The answer is mostly no. That didn’t used to matter as much as it now does. AI is already being deployed, and will increasingly be deployed, in military contexts where substantial civilian death is expected. You can write a constitution for a model. You cannot write empathy into one — empathy takes time, and it costs something to build. Paying attention is how I’ve been putting in the time.

I’m not claiming a photograph fixes any of that. But it takes time to look at something properly, and time is the thing being optimized away.

So this site keeps track. The frames worth keeping, the experiences they came from, and a journal for whatever was happening at the time.

— Cody Heart
